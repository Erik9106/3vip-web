Trademarks in Peru

How to register a trademark in Peru: requirements, costs and timelines

What you need to register a trademark in Peru, how much it costs and how long it takes. Registering a trademark has two costs: the official fee paid to INDECOPI for each class of goods or services, and the professional fees of whoever handles the procedure. Here we publish the requirements, the current fees, how the application proceeds and the reasons a trademark gets refused.

First things first: trademark or patent?

It is a very common mix-up. Many people say “patent” when they want to register a trademark, and others register a trademark believing it protects their product from copies.

I don't want anyone using my name or logo

A trademark protects the sign by which the public recognizes your goods or services: the name, the logo, or both. The exclusive right to use it is acquired through registration (art. 154). That is the procedure this page explains.

I don't want anyone copying my product

A trademark does not stop someone from making a product identical to yours under a different name. If your product is technically new, what protects it is a patent. See what it costs to patent a product →

Requirements to register a trademark

The sign must be capable of being a trademark and must not fall under any prohibition, and the application must include certain details. The articles cited are from Decision 486.

What can be registered as a trademark

Any sign capable of distinguishing goods or services in the market and of being represented graphically (art. 134), including:

  • words or combinations of words;
  • images, figures, symbols, logos, labels and emblems;
  • letters and numbers;
  • a color delimited by a shape, or a combination of colors;
  • the shape of products, their containers or packaging;
  • sounds, smells, or any combination of the above.

The sign must also be distinctive and must not resemble an earlier trademark. See why trademarks get refused →

What the application includes

The request, on a form, includes (art. 139):

  • the applicant's details: name, address and nationality or domicile, and place of incorporation if it is a company;
  • the representative's details, if one acts, and the necessary powers of attorney;
  • the trademark: the text if it is a plain word mark, or a reproduction if it has a logo, stylized lettering, shape or color;
  • the list of goods or services and the classes they belong to;
  • the signature of the applicant or representative;
  • proof of payment of the fee (art. 138).

Without the trademark, the list of goods or services, the applicant's details or proof of payment, the application is not admitted for processing and receives no filing date (art. 140).

INDECOPI fees

The fee is charged per class of the International Nice Classification in which you want to protect the trademark (art. 151). There are 45 classes: 34 for goods and 11 for services. It applies to product and service marks, collective and certification marks, trade names and commercial slogans.

Choosing the right classes matters. The trademark is protected only for the goods and services listed in the application. Filing in fewer classes than you need leaves gaps in protection; filing in classes you will not use means paying too much. To find out which class each product or service belongs to, INDECOPI publishes its own version of the Nice Classification, the PERUANIZADO.

The procedure, step by step

This is how a trademark application proceeds. The articles cited are from Andean Community Decision 486. When the law sets a time limit in days, they are working days, counted from the day after notification or publication (arts. 4 and 5).

  1. Filing the application

    The application is filed with INDECOPI's Directorate of Distinctive Signs, together with a reproduction of the mark if it has a logo, stylized lettering or colors, the list of goods or services and proof of payment of the fee.

  2. Formal examination

    The office checks that the application meets the formal requirements within the 15 working days following filing. If something is missing, it notifies the applicant, who has 60 working days to complete it; otherwise the application is deemed abandoned (art. 144).

    Legal time limit: 15 working days · Corrections: 60 working days

  3. Publication

    If the application meets the requirements, the office orders its publication (art. 145) in INDECOPI's electronic Industrial Property Gazette, so that third parties can learn of it.

  4. Opposition period

    Within the 30 working days following publication, anyone with a legitimate interest may file an opposition. On request, they are granted an additional 30 days to submit evidence (art. 146).

    If an opposition is filed, the applicant has 30 working days to answer it, extendable by a further 30 days to submit evidence (art. 148).

    Opposition: 30 working days from publication

  5. Registrability examination and decision

    Once the opposition period has expired, or the opposition has been resolved, the office examines whether the trademark can be registered and issues a decision granting or refusing registration (art. 150).

Why trademarks get refused

The fee is paid at filing, before you know whether the trademark will be granted. That is why it pays to assess the sign before paying.

It resembles an earlier trademark

A sign cannot be registered if it is identical or similar to a trademark already applied for or registered by a third party for the same or related goods or services, where it may cause confusion (art. 136). This is the most frequent cause, and it can be anticipated by searching first in Busca tu Marca.

It describes the product

Signs are not registered if they consist exclusively of the generic name of the product, indications of its quality, quantity, purpose or origin, or laudatory expressions such as “the best” (art. 135).

It is not distinctive

Nor are signs registered that lack distinctiveness, that consist of the usual shapes of the product or its packaging, or of a color on its own without a shape delimiting it (art. 135). A strong trademark is one the public associates with a single business origin.

If it is refused: trademark rescue

A refusal does not have to be the end. Assessing distinctiveness involves a degree of judgment, and it does not always take into account every applicable technical criterion. That is why a refusal can be reversed.

A request for reconsideration or an appeal can be filed against the decision within 15 working days counted from the day after notification. If you are within that period, we can help: we argue the distinctiveness of the sign on the basis of the Manual for the Examination of Trademarks in the Andean Countries to reverse the decision, or we assess other strategies for your sign.

This applies to trademarks and to distinctive signs in general, such as trade names and commercial slogans. Once the 15-day period has passed, nothing more can be done, so it is best to write to us as soon as you receive the notification.

How long does registration take?

180 days
Working days: the time limit set by
INDECOPI's TUPA for a decision

The actual length depends heavily on the examiner. Lately the office has been taking the full time limit set by the TUPA.

If that time limit expires without a decision, the applicable step is an appeal, under the General Administrative Procedure Law. In practice, this leads the Directorate of Distinctive Signs to speed up its decision, to avoid breaching the time limit.
