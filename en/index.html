The origin of the name

Veni, Vidi, Vici: I came, I saw, I conquered

n 47 BC, the landscape for the Roman Republic was critical. An ambitious king named Farnaces II had taken advantage of the chaos of the Roman civil wars to invade territories and challenge the Republic's power in the East. It was an enormous threat that jeopardized the stability of the entire region.
To stop him, the celebrated Roman general Julius Caesar marched to encounter him. The definitive showdown took place at the Battle of Zela (in modern-day Turkey). The importance of this conflict was absolute: it decided who would maintain strategic and commercial control over the area.
Caesar's response was swift and devastating. The battle lasted barely a few hours and the enemy army was completely dismantled. When reporting his triumph to the Roman Senate, Caesar did not need to send a lengthy report or beat around the bush. He summarized his feat in three powerful words that became legend: Veni, Vidi, Vici.

Three verbs. Three victories. A perfect strategy: arrive, analyze, and conquer. That same philosophy guides Veni, Vidi, Vici: we seek to understand your invention in depth, identify the right strategy to protect it, and accompany you until it is achieved.

The critical point nobody explains

Poor claim drafting can destroy your patent

Claims are the only element that legally defines what is protected in a patent. A brilliant invention with poorly drafted claims is a fortress without doors: any competitor can enter with a small change.

The protection strategy starts long before writing a single word. Our hard science background allows us to understand the invention in depth before designing its legal shield.

Has your application already received an adverse report? There is still time to respond: how we rescue a patent application.

Evaluate my claims →
✕ Weak claim — easy to work around
«Device that detects dengue using indirect sandwich ELISA with anti-NS1 monoclonal antibodies from the DENV-2 strain isolated in Lima in 2019...»

Too specific → a competitor changes the strain and no longer infringes your patent.

✓ Strategic claim — hard to work around
«Immunoenzymatic method for detection of flavivirus antigens in biological samples, comprising: (a) a solid phase functionalized with at least one capture antibody...»

Broad but anchored to real novelty → covers the entire technology family.

What is Intellectual Property?

Intellectual property protects creations of the mind. It is the legal way of telling the world 'I created this' — and of preventing others from exploiting it without your permission.

Patents

Protect new technical inventions with industrial application. Grant exclusivity for up to 20 years in exchange for publishing your invention.

Distinctive Signs

Trademarks, trade names and slogans. They distinguish your products or services in the market and are registered before INDECOPI for exclusivity.

Copyright

Protects original works: books, software, databases. It arises with creation, but registering before INDECOPI makes it easier to defend your authorship.

Plant Varieties

If you developed a new plant variety, the UPOV system grants you exclusive rights of reproduction and commercialization.

A patent is a contract with the State: you publish your invention and the State gives you a temporary monopoly to exploit it. Understanding this can transform the value of your research.

What can be patented?

Any technical invention that is new, has an inventive step and is capable of industrial application. Can be products, processes or methods.

The most common mistake

Publishing the invention before filing the patent. Once published it loses novelty and can no longer be patented. Always consult before publishing or presenting at conferences.

PCT Patent

The Patent Cooperation Treaty allows seeking protection in over 150 countries with a single initial filing. The pathway to take a Peruvian invention to the world.

How long does it take?

In Peru the process can take 3 to 5 years. From filing you have provisional protection and can indicate 'Patent Pending'.

Peru is one of the most biodiverse countries in the world. Its genetic resources have enormous scientific and economic value — but accessing them requires following international rules.

Nagoya Protocol

International agreement that regulates access to genetic resources and ensures that benefits are shared equitably with the countries and communities of origin.

Who authorizes in Peru?

Depends on the resource: Ministry of Production, SERNANP, MINAM or INIA, depending on the type of genetic resource to be researched or used.

Risks of non-compliance

Penalties, loss of rights to research results and barriers to publishing or patenting. Compliance protects both the researcher and the institution.

Foreign researchers

If you want to research using Peruvian genetic resources from abroad, you need an access contract. We can represent you and manage the entire process from Lima.
