Response to the patentability examination

Your patent application received an adverse examination report

It has not been refused yet. You have a statutory term to respond, and the quality of that response decides whether the patent is granted or lost. We have taken over that response in files started by others: all 12 cases ended in grant.

How much time you have

The term depends on what you applied for, and the difference is significant. With a utility model you have half the time of an invention patent.

Invention patent
60 + 30 working days

Sixty working days from notification. On request, extended by a further thirty working days.

Utility model
30 + 15 working days

Thirty working days from notification, extendable by fifteen further working days, once only and on request.

Halving the term is not an administrative practice: article 85 of Decision 486 provides that the rules on invention patents apply to utility models “except as regards the terms of processing, which shall be reduced by half”.

Responding is not enough

The rule does not say the patent is saved by replying within the term: it says that if despite the reply the impediments persist, the office refuses it anyway.

“The applicant shall reply to the notification within a period of sixty days following the date of notification. This period may be extended once only for a further thirty days. […] Where the applicant fails to reply within the specified period, or where despite the reply the impediments to grant persist, the competent national office shall refuse the patent.” Andean Community Decision 486, article 45

What the examination assesses

Substantive examination does not review the same things in an invention patent as in a utility model. Knowing which requirement was challenged determines how it is rebutted.

Invention patent

  • Novelty
  • Inventive step
  • Industrial application
  • Clarity of the claims
  • Sufficiency of the description

Utility model

  • Novelty
  • Technical advantage
  • Clarity of the claims
  • Sufficiency of the description

Each objection is overcome differently

Many refusals are resolved with relative ease once you understand precisely what the examiner observed. Others demand substantive technical work. These are the routes by type of objection:

Lack of clarityThe simplest

Usually a matter of drafting errors, easy to correct without touching the scope of protection.

Insufficiency of disclosureNeeds support

May require a prior art search to show that the description enables a person skilled in the art to reproduce the invention.

Lack of noveltyWork on the claims

Requires a thorough analysis of the technical features of the invention and expressing them correctly in the claim, so the difference from what is already disclosed becomes clear.

Lack of inventive stepThe most demanding argument

You must identify the features that make the invention non-obvious to a person normally skilled in the art, and sustain it with technical argument.

Lack of technical advantageUtility models only

Consists of finding and evidencing the technical features that provide an advantage over the closest invention in the prior art.

In many of these cases it is necessary to rely on the Andean Community patent examination guidelines, which set the criteria the examiner applies. Arguing from that framework, rather than from intuition, is what separates a reply that succeeds from one that does not.

How we handle your case

We read the report before accepting the engagement

We review the notification, the cited prior art and the application as filed. That yields an honest assessment: which objections can be overcome, which cannot, and with what realistic scope a grant might be achieved.

We set the strategy and the room for amendment

We decide what is rebutted by technical argument and what requires reformulating claims, taking care not to concede scope unnecessarily or introduce matter not originally disclosed.

We draft the response

Argument directed at each specific objection raised by the examiner, grounded in the original description, the prior art and the Andean examination guidelines, with the claim set adjusted where appropriate.

We stay with the file until resolution

Article 45 allows the office to issue two or more notifications. If a further requirement arrives, the defence continues along the same line of argument.
